Eunice Shin

Development economics · Nighttime lights and satellite data · Law and public policy

I hold an honors B.A. in economics from UC Berkeley. My research interests are in development economics and the economic effects of laws, institutions, and public policy. I’m particularly interested in using satellite observations of nighttime lights to measure local economic activity where conventional data are limited.

My honors thesis examined the relationship between historical slave exports and present-day nighttime lights across African ethnic homelands. It received UC Berkeley’s Mark A. Schimbor Prize in Economics. I also collaborate with Jaecheol Lee at Sogang University on juvenile justice and judicial outcomes in Korea.

Research

Honors thesis · 2026

Slave Trade’s Shadow: Historical Slave Exports and Nighttime Lights

I built a dataset linking African ethnic homelands to historical slave exports, geography, and satellite observations. In my cross-sectional estimates, coastal proximity had a stronger association with lights per capita than historical slave exports.

Mark A. Schimbor Prize in Economics, UC Berkeley

Pointing to the Price of Punishment: The Impact of California’s 1994 Three Strikes Law on Imprisonment

I used synthetic control and difference-in-differences to study arrests, incarceration, and corrections spending, and examined how donor fit affected what the analysis could establish.
